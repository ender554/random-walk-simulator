# Monty Hall: feature research (2026-10-28)

**How to be the best:** the one page that settles the argument for a skeptic. Let people play, then see their own games broken down case by case, and let them change the host's rules (knows / doesn't know / lazy) to watch exactly when switching stops helping.

Competitors:
- UCSD has an ignorant-host mode.
- Coding Horror covers the Monty Fall and Monty Crawl variants.
- The Scientific American interactive lets you play the host. It cites a study in which taking the host's view converts people.
- Coatless Professor switches from manual play to auto runs.
- A Mendeley tool has speed control and a biased host.
- Philosophy Experiments has crowd tallies.
- The SticiGui and CAUSE versions are dead Java.

What people say finally convinced them:
- running a simulation
- the 100-door version
- grouping "your door vs. the rest"
- listing all three cases
- taking the host's view

Small personal samples mislead: one player who stayed 12 times said it "sure does seem like 50/50".

Must:
- A host-rule selector: knows, doesn't know (car reveals voided), lazy.
- A case table or tree built from your own games.
- A 2/3 grouping visual.
- An auto-play race between always-stay and always-switch.
- Play-as-host mode.

Should:
- A small-sample warning on your tally.
- A 100-door preset.
- A share link.
- Class tally codes.

Could:
- Explain-as-you-go captions.
- CSV export.
- A predict-then-test quiz.
