# Coin Flip Simulator: feature research (2026-10-28)

**How to be the best:** take a learner from one satisfying flip to a visible H/T sequence and then to a million-flip proof of the law of large numbers. Show how the randomness works, and expose people's intuition about streaks with a real-vs-fake game, streak checks and binomial checks, all on one seeded, shareable page.

Competitors:
- Coatless Professor has a visible sequence, streak charts and a z-score.
- uplup and random.org flip multiple coins, keep a log and export CSV.
- Omni has an exact P(k heads) calculator.
- flip-a-coin.com has a binomial chart, a walk with ±2σ, a streak distribution, an embed, and states its random number generator.
- Inventive HQ has step, pause and speed controls.
- Nobody has a playable real-vs-fake game.

Evidence:
- Skeptics doubt that online flips are random.
- The Bartoš et al. study of 350,757 flips found a 50.8% same-side bias.
- Teachers spot invented 100-flip sequences because they lack long runs (Arizona handout).

Must:
- Flip 1, 10 and 100 buttons with coin animation.
- A sequence strip showing streaks.
- The real-vs-fake game.
- A share link.
- A note on the randomness, plus a "real coins" fact panel.

Should:
- A binomial panel against the exact PMF.
- An exact probability calculator.
- A run-length histogram.
- CSV export.
- Playback.

Could:
- A heads-minus-tails view with a √n band.
- Multiple coins per toss.
- Optional sound.
